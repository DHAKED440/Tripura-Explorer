Multi-Objective Vector Optimization

Balancing Multi-Objective Route Trade-offs & Pareto Dominance

Real road journeys must simultaneously balance conflicting criteria: minimizing driving time, minimizing distance, minimizing financial expenditure, and maximizing scenic or cultural enjoyment. We model this as vector optimization over graph paths \( \mathcal{P} \).

1. Vector Problem Statement

\[ \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \; \mathbf{F}(P) = \big( f_1(P), f_2(P), \dots, f_m(P) \big) \]
Where: \( P \in \mathcal{P} \) is an admissible highway path between origin and destination; \( \mathbf{F}(P) \) is the objective vector containing distance \( f_1 \), travel time \( f_2 \), toll/fuel cost \( f_3 \), and inverted scenic score \( f_4 \).
Intuition: No single path simultaneously minimizes all goals; we must optimize the multi-dimensional vector.

2. Weighted-Sum Scalarization

\[ F(P) = \sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i \tilde f_i(P), \quad \sum_{i=1}^{m} w_i = 1,\; w_i \ge 0 \]
Where: \( w_i \) represents user-selected preference weights; \( \tilde f_i \) are unitless normalized metrics constrained to sum strictly to \( 1.0 \).
Intuition: Converts multiple competing goals into a convex scalar score based on traveler priorities.

3. Metric Normalization

\[ \tilde f_i(P) = \dfrac{f_i(P) - f_i^{\min}}{f_i^{\max} - f_i^{\min}} \]
Where: Prevents dimensional skew when summing incompatible units (e.g., kilometers, minutes, Indian Rupees).
Intuition: Maps all metrics onto the uniform domain \( [0, 1] \) so weights retain equal relative influence.

4. Pareto Dominance Condition

\[ P_1 \prec P_2 \iff \forall i: f_i(P_1) \le f_i(P_2) \ \wedge\ \exists j: f_j(P_1) < f_j(P_2) \]
Where: Path \( P_1 \) strictly dominates \( P_2 \) if \( P_1 \) is no worse in any objective and strictly better in at least one.
Intuition: A dominated route is irrational to choose because another route is strictly superior without sacrifice.

5. \(\varepsilon\)-Constraint Method

\[ \min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} f_1(P) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad f_j(P) \le \varepsilon_j \;\; (\forall j \ne 1) \]
Where: Optimizes primary travel time \( f_1 \) while bounding secondary goals (e.g., maximum budget \( \le \varepsilon_2 \)).
Intuition: Discovers non-convex Pareto frontier points that weighted-sum scalarization might overlook.

6. Tourist Mode Dynamic Weights

\[ S(v) = w_i I(v) + w_p P(v) + w_f F(v) - w_t T(v) \]
Where: Tourist Mode scores destinations using theme match \( I \), popularity \( P \), data verification \( F \), and detour time \( T \).
Intuition: Blends mathematical graph efficiency with tourist enjoyment and cultural preferences.

Theoretical Foundation

Mathematical Foundations of Routing

Complete textbook reference of formulas, relaxation criteria, and computational complexities driving every route calculation in Tripura Explorer.

Table of Contents 1. Graph Definition \( G=(V,E) \) 2. Path Cost Metric \( w(P) \) 3. Dijkstra Relaxation \( d[v] \) 4. A* Search \( f=g+h \) 5. Bellman-Ford DP \( d_k[v] \) 6. Kruskal MST DSU 7. TSP & 2-Opt Held-Karp 8. Haversine Distance \( d_{\text{sph}} \) 9. Complexity Benchmarks \( \mathcal{O} \)

1. Graph Topology Formulation

\[ G = (V, E), \quad w : E \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \]
Where:
  • \( V \): Set of destination vertices (destinations in Tripura, e.g. Agartala, Udaipur, Unakoti, Neermahal).
  • \( E \subseteq V \times V \): Set of authenticated highway corridors connecting destinations.
  • \( w(e) \ge 0 \): Non-negative weight mapping each edge to physical cost (travel time in minutes, road km, or INR).
Tripura Example: \( |V| = 16 \) verified destinations, \( |E| = 25 \) national/state highway corridors, average vertex degree \( \bar{d} = \frac{2|E|}{|V|} = \frac{50}{16} = 3.13 \).
Intuition: Represents physical geography as a mathematical sparse planar network where exact traversal theorems hold.

2. Aggregate Path Cost

\[ w(P) = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} w(v_i, v_{i+1}) \]
Where:
  • \( P = (v_1, v_2, \dots, v_k) \): Sequence of vertices forming a continuous journey path.
  • \( w(v_i, v_{i+1}) \): Weight of the individual corridor leg between stop \( v_i \) and \( v_{i+1} \).
  • \( w(P) \): Total cumulative journey burden (additive metric).
Tripura Example: For path \( P = (\text{Agartala} \to \text{Bishalgarh} \to \text{Udaipur}) \): \( w(P) = 22.0\text{ km} + 29.2\text{ km} = 51.2\text{ km} \), driving time \( = 35\text{ min} + 40\text{ min} = 75\text{ min} \).
Intuition: Road travel cost is strictly additive along traversed highway segments without teleportation.

3. Dijkstra Edge Relaxation Recurrence

\[ d[v] \leftarrow \min\big\{ d[v],\; d[u] + w(u, v) \big\} \]
Where:
  • \( d[u] \): Minimum known distance from origin hub \( s \) to vertex \( u \).
  • \( w(u, v) \): Weight of highway connection from \( u \) to candidate neighbor \( v \).
  • \( d[v] \): Tentative distance to \( v \), updated whenever a shorter route through \( u \) is discovered.
Tripura Example: Current tentative distance to Udaipur is \( d[\text{Udaipur}] = 85\text{ min} \). Exploring via Bishalgarh where \( d[\text{Bishalgarh}] = 35\text{ min} \) and \( w(\text{Bishalgarh}, \text{Udaipur}) = 40\text{ min} \): updated \( d[\text{Udaipur}] \leftarrow \min(85, 35 + 40) = 75\text{ min} \).
Intuition: Greedily extracts the closest unvisited vertex from a min-heap; once extracted, its distance is mathematically guaranteed to be minimal.

4. A* Evaluation & Admissibility Guarantee

\[ f(n) = g(n) + h(n), \quad \text{with} \quad h(n) \le h^*(n) \quad \text{and} \quad h(u) \le w(u, v) + h(v) \]
Where:
  • \( g(n) \): Exact road cost from source \( s \) to node \( n \) discovered so far.
  • \( h(n) \): Heuristic estimate from node \( n \) to goal target \( t \).
  • \( f(n) \): Total estimated journey cost through node \( n \).
  • Admissibility (\( h(n) \le h^*(n) \)): The heuristic never overestimates the true remaining distance.
  • Consistency / Monotonicity: Triangle inequality holds: \( h(u) \le w(u, v) + h(v) \).
Tripura Example: Driving from Agartala to Unakoti. True road distance is \( 178\text{ km} \). Spherical Haversine straight-line distance is \( 108.4\text{ km} \). Because \( 108.4 \le 178 \), the heuristic is strictly admissible!
Intuition: A straight line on Earth's surface is always shorter than winding road corridors; therefore, A* never prunes the true optimal route.

5. Bellman-Ford Dynamic Programming Recurrence

\[ d_k[v] = \min\Big( d_{k-1}[v],\; \min_{(u, v) \in E} \big( d_{k-1}[u] + w(u, v) \big) \Big) \]
Where:
  • \( d_k[v] \): Shortest distance from source \( s \) to \( v \) using at most \( k \) corridor edges.
  • Repeats for \( k = 1, 2, \dots, |V| - 1 \).
  • Negative Cycle Check: If \( d_{|V|}[v] < d_{|V|-1}[v] \) on pass \( |V| \), an absorbing negative cycle exists.
Tripura Example: Across \( |V| = 16 \) nodes, Bellman-Ford performs up to 15 relaxation sweeps over all 25 edges, terminating early when no distance improves.
Intuition: A simple path can contain at most \( |V|-1 \) edges. Checking all edges in each pass guarantees global convergence even if edge costs vary.

6. Kruskal's Minimum Spanning Tree (MST)

\[ \min_{T \subseteq E} \sum_{e \in T} w(e) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad (V, T) \text{ is connected and acyclic} \]
Where:
  • \( T \): Tree subset of edges containing exactly \( |V| - 1 \) corridors connecting all destinations.
  • Cut Property: The minimum-weight edge crossing any cut in \( G \) must belong to the MST.
  • Uses Disjoint-Set Union (DSU) with near-constant \( \mathcal{O}(\alpha(|V|)) \) cycle checking.
Tripura Example: Connects all 16 major cultural destinations with exactly \( 15 \) highway links totaling \( 412.5\text{ km} \), minimizing total infrastructure cost.
Intuition: Greedily add the lightest road corridor in the state that doesn't create a loop until all destinations are united.

7. Travelling Salesperson (TSP) & 2-Opt Local Search

\[ C(S, j) = \min_{i \in S \setminus \{j\}} \big[ C(S \setminus \{j\}, i) + d(i, j) \big] \]
Where:
  • \( C(S, j) \): Minimum cost of a path visiting all vertices in subset \( S \) exactly once, starting at origin hub and ending at vertex \( j \).
  • 2-Opt Swap Criterion: A pair of edges \( (u_1, v_1) \) and \( (u_2, v_2) \) is replaced by \( (u_1, u_2) \) and \( (v_1, v_2) \) whenever \( d(u_1, u_2) + d(v_1, v_2) < d(u_1, v_1) + d(u_2, v_2) \).
Tripura Example: A 4-stop tour: Agartala \(\to\) Sepahijala \(\to\) Neermahal \(\to\) Udaipur \(\to\) Agartala. 2-Opt eliminates highway criss-crossing, saving 28 km of unnecessary backtracking.
Intuition: Untangles crossing paths on the road map by reversing sub-tours that reduce total distance.

8. Geodesic Haversine Distance Formula

\[ d = 2R \arcsin\left( \sqrt{\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\phi}{2}\right) + \cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2\sin^2\left(\frac{\Delta\lambda}{2}\right)} \right) \]
Where:
  • \( \phi_1, \phi_2 \): Geodetic latitudes of source and destination in radians.
  • \( \lambda_1, \lambda_2 \): Geodetic longitudes in radians (\( \Delta\phi = \phi_2 - \phi_1 \), \( \Delta\lambda = \lambda_2 - \lambda_1 \)).
  • \( R = 6,371.0\text{ km} \): Earth's mean volumetric radius.
Tripura Example: Agartala (\( 23.8364^\circ\text{N}, 91.2775^\circ\text{E} \)) to Udaipur (\( 23.5337^\circ\text{N}, 91.4817^\circ\text{E} \)): \( \Delta\phi = -0.3027^\circ \), \( \Delta\lambda = +0.2042^\circ \). Great-circle distance \( d = 39.4\text{ km} \). Real road distance via NH-8 is \( 51.2\text{ km} \).
Intuition: Calculates true great-circle spherical distance over Earth's curvature without map projection distortion.
