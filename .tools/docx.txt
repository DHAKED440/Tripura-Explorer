Prepared 9 October 2026 · Based on the 6-screen prototype (Home, Explore, Destination Details, Map Planner, Trip Planner, Mobile)

1. Purpose and summary
This report lists every piece of data the Tripura Tourism Explorer prototype needs, the exact fields to collect, where to collect each one, and how to keep the data trustworthy. The prototype is a content-heavy product: almost every screen is driven by a destination record, so the quality of that record decides the quality of the whole app.
Key findings
The app needs seven data groups: destinations, categories and districts, media, location and map data, visitor information, experiences (festivals, crafts, food), and user-generated data (reviews, favourites, trips).
Most factual data should come from official sources (Tripura Tourism, the Forest Department, the Archaeological Survey of India, district administrations) and be confirmed by a field visit.
Several values in the prototype (entry fee, timings, district names, review counts) are placeholders and must not go live until verified.
The district filter in the prototype lists only 6 districts. Tripura has 8, so Sepahijala and Khowai are missing.
Reviews, ratings and trip plans cannot be bought or copied. They must be generated by your own users after launch.

3.2 Categories and districts
Categories: Nature, Heritage, Hills, Lakes, Temples, Culture. Add Wildlife, which appears on the Map Planner but not on the Home chips.
Districts: Tripura has 8 districts: West Tripura, Sepahijala, Khowai, Gomati, South Tripura, Dhalai, Unakoti and North Tripura. Add the missing two to the filter.
Keep a district master table and link every destination to it by ID, not by typed text, so filters never break.

3.6 Local Experiences
The navigation bar has a Local Experiences section, so plan content for:
Festivals and events: name, month, location, community, short description (for example Garia Puja, Kharchi Puja, Durga Puja in Agartala, Ker Puja).
Crafts: bamboo and cane products, handloom (Risa and Pachra), where to buy, price range.
Food: Mui Borok dishes, Berma, local restaurants.
Cultural performances and communities: short, respectful descriptions reviewed by community members.
Guides and homestays: name, contact, languages, price, verification.

Collection methods
Official request: write to the tourism department and district offices for approved fact sheets and photo libraries.
Field survey: a team visits each place with a standard form (Section 7) to record coordinates, photos, fees and facilities.
Desk research: government websites, gazetteers and published research to write descriptions.
Community input: local guides and cultural experts review festival and community content.
Public datasets and APIs: OpenStreetMap for geometry, climate normals for seasons. Read each licence first. Scraping reviews or photos from commercial sites is not allowed by most terms of service.
5. Starting list of destinations
The prototype shows six places. For a useful launch, collect 30 to 40. Start here and confirm each one:
In the prototype: Neermahal, Unakoti, Jampui Hills, Dumboor Lake, Tripura Sundari Temple, Sipahijala Wildlife Sanctuary.
Candidates to add: Ujjayanta Palace and the State Museum (Agartala), Heritage Park, Pilak, Debtamura, Boxanagar, Kamalasagar, Trishna Wildlife Sanctuary, Gumti Wildlife Sanctuary.
6. Items to verify before launch
These values in the prototype look like placeholders or may be wrong. Treat them as open questions.
District labels: Neermahal (Melaghar) and Sipahijala Wildlife Sanctuary appear to fall in Sepahijala district, not West Tripura or Gomati. Confirm Dumboor Lake's district as well. Use official records.
Entry fee, timings and closed day on the Neermahal page.
Review count and ratings (4.8, 124 reviews). These cannot exist before users write reviews. Hide them or show "New" until real data arrives.
"Verified" badge: only show it when a named person has checked the record, and show the date.
Spelling: the prototype uses "Sipahijala" while the district is written "Sepahijala". Pick one standard spelling for each name.
7. Field survey form
Use this checklist for every site visit:
Official name, local name and spelling variants
GPS coordinates of the main entrance and of the main attraction
District, subdivision, nearest town
Ticket prices (Indian, foreign, children, camera, vehicle) with a photo of the price board
Opening hours, closed days, seasonal changes
Facilities (parking, toilets, food, drinking water, boat, guide, ATM, mobile network)
Accessibility and difficulty
Time needed for a visit
At least 10 photos (wide, detail, people, access road, signboards)
Name and phone of the person who confirmed the information, and the date
Safety and rule notes
Nearby places and typical road time
8. Data quality and legal rules
Source tracking: every record stores its source, who collected it and the last-verified date. Re-verify fees and timings every 6 months.
Single source of truth: keep all data in one database or content system. Do not type facts separately into each page.
Image rights: keep a written licence or permission for each photo. Never use images from a search engine without a licence.
Reviews: moderate for abuse and fake content. Allow only logged-in users to post.
User privacy: collect only what is needed, state the purpose, and follow India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 for accounts, location and trip data.
Community content: get approval from community representatives before publishing cultural descriptions or photos of people.
Language: plan English first. Add Bengali and Kokborok later; collect the translated names early.
